There is no official Big Ambitions wiki — the developer, Hovgaard Games, publishes information through its own website, Steam announcements, Discord and forum instead. What exists is a set of community wikis and fan sites of very different ages and quality, plus this one, which focuses on the questions players actually search with data verified against the 1.0 release. This page maps the whole landscape so you can pick the right resource for the right question.

The short version: use the Fandom wiki for broad encyclopedic lookups, use dedicated fan sites for tools and niche guides, use official channels for patch news, and use this wiki for verified, dated answers to high-traffic questions like the [best business](/best-business), [cinema layouts](/cinema-layout) or the [happiness system](/happiness).

## The community wikis available today

The Fandom wiki at big-ambitions.fandom.com is the largest and oldest community reference. It covers buildings, employees, businesses, the radio song list and game systems in encyclopedia style. Its weakness is age: a meaningful share of its pages were written during Early Access and have not been revisited since the 1.0 balance passes, so numbers can lag the live game.

A second wiki exists on the wiki.gg platform, with pages like requirements and location lists that are newer in places than the Fandom equivalents. Around them sits a constellation of small fan sites — biggerambitions.com, which hosts genuinely useful tools like a factory builder and blueprint help pages, and several smaller guide sites of varying depth. Quality among the small sites varies wildly; some are thorough, others are thin keyword pages. As of 2026, none of the community wikis fully reflects the 1.0 release yet.

## The official sources worth bookmarking

Because there is no official wiki, Hovgaard Games spreads real information across four places. The official website, bigambitionsgame.com, carries game info, a news page and the living roadmap image that tracks updates from EA 0.8 through 1.0 and the announced Costa Del Sol DLC. Steam announcements are where releases and patch notes actually post, so the Steam community hub is the fastest way to see what changed.

The official Discord (discord.gg/hovgaardgames) is where the developer communicates day to day, and where community projects — including the unofficial multiplayer mod — coordinate. Finally, the official forum at forum.bigambitionsgame.com hosts longer developer posts and feedback threads, including an active mods section. If a question is about the future of the game, one of these four will answer it before any wiki does.

## How this wiki fits in

This site was built around a simple observation: the highest-traffic Big Ambitions questions — the best business to run, where to buy specific items, how happiness works, whether there is multiplayer — had answers scattered across Reddit threads, Steam guides and half-updated wikis, often contradicting each other. So we took those questions, researched each one across official sources, Steam data, Reddit and community guides, and wrote one dated, verified page per question.

Every article here shows its last-updated date and the version of the game it was checked against. Where numbers are community reports rather than official values, we say so; where something is genuinely unknown, we write "to be confirmed" instead of inventing an answer. When patch news lands, our [roadmap](/roadmap) and [1.0 release](/big-ambitions-1-0) pages are the first places we update.

## How this wiki verifies its pages

Because this site's whole pitch is "checked, dated answers", the method deserves a paragraph. Each article starts from a real search players make, then gathers every source that claims an answer: the official site and roadmap, Steam store data and community guides, developer posts on the forum and social channels, and the Reddit threads where players compare results. Claims that survive cross-checking get written up; claims that appear in only one place are marked as such.

Numbers get extra care. Player counts and review totals come from Steam's own data, prices from store trackers, and in-game figures like profit margins are always labeled as community reports rather than official values. When a fact cannot be verified — a system requirement we have not re-checked, a changelog line still buried in an announcement — the page says "to be confirmed" instead of filling the gap with something plausible. It is a slower way to write a wiki, and it is the reason pages here carry dates the size of a headline.

## Choosing the right resource for your question

A practical rule of thumb: encyclopedic lookups first, verification second. For "what items exist" or "how does business type X work in outline", the Fandom wiki is still the fastest reference. For "is this number still true in 1.0" or "what should I actually do", prefer dated guides — here or elsewhere — that show their sources and update dates.

And for anything official — release dates, patch notes, DLC plans — go straight to the horse: the official site, Steam announcements, Discord and forum. Community wikis are wonderful, but they are interpretation layers, and during patch weeks the interpretation layer is always the last thing to update. Bookmark both kinds of source and Big Ambitions will stop surprising you in expensive ways.

## A note on dates

The most useful habit on any game wiki is reading the date before the article. Guides written during Early Access can be perfectly correct about systems that never changed and perfectly wrong about numbers that have. Every article on this site carries its last-updated date and the game version it was checked against — 1.0, checked September 2026 for the launch batch — and pages that depend on volatile data, like [prices](/price) and the [roadmap](/roadmap), say so explicitly. If a page has no date, treat its numbers as decoration.
