The right cinema layout in Big Ambitions starts with one rule: blueprints are tied to building sizes. A blueprint saved for one building type only fits buildings with the same size and layout — an A1 blueprint cannot be applied to a different premises class. Almost every "my blueprint won't load" problem comes from violating that rule, so check the building class before you fall in love with any layout you found online.

With that fixed in mind, a good cinema is about flow, not furniture. Customers arrive, buy tickets, buy concessions, find their screen and leave; every bottleneck in that chain becomes lost sales and a worse review. This page covers how blueprints work, the layout priorities that matter for cinemas specifically, and the mistakes that most often sink a first theater.

## How blueprints work in 1.0

Blueprints are Big Ambitions' system for saving and reusing interior designs, and since the blueprint editor arrived in Early Access 0.10 they have been creatable directly from the main menu with what the developer calls complete creative freedoms — you no longer need to own a matching store just to design one. You design walls, floors and furniture, save the result as a blueprint, and apply it to any building of the same size class.

The blueprint editor pairs with the interior designer hand tool, also from 0.10, which lets you pack, unpack, place and rotate objects directly in the interior view. Community blueprints circulate on the Steam Workshop, and applying someone else's design to your cinema is a legitimate shortcut — as long as the size class matches and you still stock the businesses' required items yourself.

One workflow tip from players who lean on blueprints: build one cinema layout you like, save it, and reuse it for every future cinema in the same class. Standardizing also standardizes your costs, your stocking list and your staff planning, which is exactly how the game's best players expand without chaos.

## Designing the flow: lobby, concessions, screens

Think of a cinema as three zones, and lay them out in the order customers experience them. The lobby is where first impressions and lines form — keep it open near the entrance, because crowding at the door is the first failure of bad cinema layouts. The concession stand belongs between the entrance and the screens, positioned so the pre-show queue never blocks the lobby or the entrance to a screening room; concessions are where a cinema actually makes its margin, so burying the stand is burying your profit.

The screening rooms themselves are straightforward by comparison: screens at the back of the premises, seating arranged for clear sightlines, and enough spacing that staff can clean between shows. Remember that in Big Ambitions staff physically walk these routes — a back-of-house path that lets employees restock concessions and clean rooms without crossing the customer queue is the difference between a cinema that runs itself and one that stalls every showtime.

Finally, respect the fixed points every cinema has to include: the screen, projection-related equipment and the customer facilities your business type requires. Food-and-drink rules bite entertainment venues too — if your cinema sells anything edible, think through where customers will consume it, because our [bathroom stall guide](/where-to-buy-bathroom-stall) explains how quickly facility requirements catch up with seating counts.

## Staffing and stocking your layout

A layout is only as good as the roster running it. Cinemas need coverage across ticket sales, concessions and room upkeep, and since showtimes cluster, your schedule needs more staff on peak evenings than on dead afternoons — the BizMan scheduler exists precisely for this. Understaffed concessions during a premier night is lost margin you cannot recover later.

Stocking is the quieter failure. Concession stands need product, and a cinema far from your warehouses means manual restocking runs; this is the point where cinema owners usually graduate to the logistics systems covered in our [warehouse](/warehouse) guide. Until then, place your cinema where a delivery route can reach it, or accept the errands.

## Pricing, showtimes and the daily rhythm

A cinema's layout shapes traffic, but pricing and scheduling decide what that traffic is worth. Ticket prices interact with your store's appeal and the neighborhood's wealth — the same pricing-by-district logic that governs retail — while concession items carry the fat margins, so the stand's price list deserves your attention before tickets do. The community's consistent advice is to treat concessions as the profit center and tickets as the traffic source.

Showtimes are the other half of the rhythm. Screenings cluster customers into waves, which is why the schedule determines staffing peaks and cleaning windows; a cinema with staggered start times spreads its queues and needs less lobby. Match showtimes to the district's life — evening crowds near offices, weekend matinees in residential areas — and the same building earns meaningfully more without moving a single wall.

## Common cinema mistakes

Three failures account for most bad first cinemas. Building class mismatch — buying a blueprint for the wrong premises size and discovering it will not apply. Concession starvation — a beautiful lobby with a tiny, badly placed stand that cannot absorb the pre-show rush. And staffing by optimism — one employee trying to sell tickets, pour drinks and clean rooms at the same time.

The fix for all three is the same discipline: verify the building class before designing, walk your customer's path from street to seat before opening, and watch one full showtime from the lobby with an honest eye. ## Finding community blueprints

You do not have to design alone. The Steam Workshop hosts player-made blueprints for cinemas and theaters, installable in-game through the interior installation firms, and the subreddit regularly carries layout posts where players share floor plans and the reasoning behind them — the blueprint-focused playstyle threads are especially useful for seeing how experienced owners zone a screen-and-lobby building before spending a dollar.

Two checks before applying any downloaded blueprint: confirm the building size class matches yours, and read the item list so you know what stock and staff the design assumes.
