The games most often recommended as "like Big Ambitions" are Startup Company, Software Inc., Another Brick in the Mall, Project Hospital, Planet Coaster, Capitalism Lab, Mashinky and Restaurant Empire 2. That list comes from cross-checking community threads on Reddit with the algorithmic similarity rankings on IGDB and SteamPeek, and it holds up — but "similar" means different things for each one, so this page groups them by what part of Big Ambitions they actually replicate.

The framing matters because Big Ambitions is genuinely hard to match. Its particular mix — a walkable open-world New York, character life-sim elements, and store-by-store business management — has no true clone. Every game below captures a slice: some nail the empire-building economy, some nail the operations, some nail the role-playing around the business.

## Closest overall: Startup Company and Software Inc.

Startup Company is the most personal recommendation possible: it is by Hovgaard Games, the same Danish developer, and it shares Big Ambitions' DNA of building an organization from nothing into a serious enterprise. Where Big Ambitions has you running stores, Startup Company has you running a software company — hiring developers, building a product, competing in a market. It is completed, affordable and the gentlest entry point on this list.

Software Inc. takes the "build a company, manage employees, ship products" loop deeper: construct an office building, staff it with specialists with individual needs and skills, and navigate projects and markets over years. It trades Big Ambitions' city walking for organizational depth, and it is the community's standing answer for players who loved the management half of the game most. Both are single-player, like Big Ambitions — no multiplayer in any of this trio.

## Store and mall operations: Another Brick in the Mall

If what hooked you was the interior itself — shelving, queues, checkout throughput — Another Brick in the Mall is the community's direct pointer. You design a mall: place stores, arrange shelves and aisles, manage stockrooms, loading bays and staff, and watch customer flow expose every layout mistake. It is the most granular "retail operations" game on this list, and it is unsentimental about logistics in a way Big Ambitions players will find familiar.

The trade-off is scope and presentation: it is a grid-based construction-and-management game, not a lived-in city with a character who needs sleep. Where Big Ambitions abstracts supply chains into a pleasant mid-game, Another Brick in the Mall makes logistics the whole game. Love your warehouses more than your storefronts? That is your next hundred hours.

## Theme-park scale tycoons: Planet Coaster, Project Hospital, Mashinky

Planet Coaster (and its sibling Planet Zoo) represents the polished modern tycoon in the lineage of the genre's classics: design-driven attractions, guest happiness as a resource, and financial management with training wheels off. It captures the "watch customers experience something you built" joy of Big Ambitions' stores, at theme-park scale, with production values few sims match.

Project Hospital is the operations purist's pick — a hospital you architect department by department, staffed by doctors with specializations, treating patients whose conditions flow through your system like customers through a store. And Mashinky covers transport tycoon: build routes, move goods, evolve through eras. Neither resembles Big Ambitions on the surface, but both scratch the same "design a machine of people and watch it run" itch, which is why they recur in every recommendation thread.

## The deep end and the odd one out: Capitalism Lab and Restaurant Empire 2

Capitalism Lab is the grand-strategy end of business simulation: corporations, stocks, banking, marketing, R&D and global markets, with none of Big Ambitions' role-playing wrapper. It is the game for players who discovered they care about the economic empire more than the city it lives in — and it is famously deep, with expansions on top of an already dense base game.

Restaurant Empire 2 is the list's curiosity: a restaurant tycoon with light RPG elements — a named protagonist, a story, scoped scenarios — which is the closest structural cousin to Big Ambitions' "one person builds a hospitality empire" fantasy, albeit with far older presentation. Between these two and the rest of the list, every flavor of "more Big Ambitions" exists except the one thing no competitor offers: Hovgaard's New York itself. ## How to choose your next game

With the list grouped by what it copies, picking is a two-question exercise. First, which part of Big Ambitions did you actually play most? If your evenings went to redesigning store interiors and squeezing margins, the operations games (Another Brick in the Mall, Project Hospital) will feel like home. If you loved the empire arc — one shop becoming a corporation — stay with the company builders (Startup Company, Software Inc., Capitalism Lab), which trade the city for scale.

Second, how much roughness do you tolerate? Planet Coaster and Restaurant Empire 2 are finished, polished products you can recommend to anyone; Software Inc. and Another Brick in the Mall are deeper but demand more patience with systems and interfaces. And if the honest answer is that what you loved was New York itself — the walkable city, the life around the businesses — then no game on this list replaces that, and the 1.0 release covered in our [1.0 roundup](/big-ambitions-1-0) is the strongest argument for simply playing more Big Ambitions.


## Honorable mentions from the community lists

Beyond the headline picks, the same recommendation threads keep surfacing a second ring of titles. From IGDB's similarity ranking: Project Aura, Computer Tycoon and TransOcean — The Shipping Company for players drawn to the economic-planning side of Big Ambitions. From the tycoon threads more broadly: Two Point Hospital for accessible operations with charm, and the Tropico series for running a whole economy with personality rather than a single storefront.

None of these will feel like a Big Ambitions sequel the way Startup Company does in spirit, but as a second game after this one, every title above has earned its community. The full ranked lists on IGDB and SteamPeek run to twenty or thirty entries if you would rather browse than choose.
