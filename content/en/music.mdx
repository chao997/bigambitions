Music in Big Ambitions works in two separate layers, and most confusion comes from mixing them. The first layer is diegetic: a store plays music to its customers through a store stereo you buy and place inside the shop, which nudges customer happiness while they browse. The second layer is yours: the game lets you add your own MP3 files as a custom radio station, played in your stores and vehicles from the same in-game radio selector.

Both layers are cheap to set up and genuinely worth doing — background music is one of the small interior-quality touches that makes a store feel finished rather than functional. This page covers buying and placing the stereo, selecting stations, and the exact custom-music workflow, which is the same on Windows and macOS.

## In-store music: buy the stereo first

Stores do not make sound on their own; you furnish them. The store stereo (and loudspeaker-style options) is sold at furniture stores — the same shops that stock bathroom fixtures and commercial fittings, covered in our [bathroom stall guide](/where-to-buy-bathroom-stall). If you cannot find the nearest furniture shop, the in-game phone's Voogle-style map has a furniture store filter that marks them for you.

Once bought, place the stereo in build mode somewhere sensible for shoppers — near the sales floor rather than the stockroom — and it starts broadcasting whichever radio station is selected. The game's own radio stations play the licensed soundtrack the community wiki documents in full, so even without any custom files, a stereo turns a silent shop into a lively one and feeds the customer-experience side of your interior quality.

Selecting the station happens from your character's phone rather than the stereo itself: open the phone, pick the radio station, and every equipped venue follows your choice. It is a small detail, but it means one tap changes the soundtrack of your whole empire.

## Custom music: your MP3s as a radio station

The custom-music flow is built into the options menu, and it is identical across platforms. Open the Options menu, go to Audio, and click the "open folder" button — this opens a music folder on your PC. Drop your MP3 files into that folder, return to the game, and hit refresh on the station list; your files now appear as a custom radio station selectable on your phone, in your stores and in your vehicles.

Supported formats are the ordinary audio files players already have — MP3 being the community-standard choice. There is no in-game editor, no playlist builder and no per-store station assignment: every store and vehicle shares the station selector, and your custom station sits in the list alongside the built-in ones. If your custom station does not show up, the fix is the refresh button — files added to the folder while the station list was already loaded need the list reloaded.

One practical note: the music folder is per-computer, not part of your save. Steam Cloud syncs your saves (with a quota of roughly 4.7 GB), but your custom soundtrack lives on the machine where you put it, so reinstalling or switching PCs means re-adding the folder's contents.

## Why bother: the gameplay payoff

The gameplay effect of store music is customer experience. Background music contributes to the atmosphere customers feel in your stores — part of the same interior-quality family as furniture, decoration and the cleanliness factors covered in our [happiness guide](/happiness). It is not a replacement for pricing well or staffing properly, but it is nearly free after the one-time stereo purchase, and "nearly free happiness" is the best margin in the game.

There is also the honest, non-economic answer: running a fashion boutique in Midtown with your own soundtrack playing is exactly the lifestyle fantasy the game sells, and 1.0's added amenities — from the personal driver to The Hamptons mansion — make the city worth a soundtrack. The full in-game track list is documented on the community wiki if you want to know what your customers are humming before your own playlist takes over.

## The built-in radio: what plays by default

Before touching custom files, it is worth knowing what the game ships with. Big Ambitions includes its own radio stations with a licensed indie soundtrack, and the community wiki documents the station and song list in full — a rarity for the genre and a sign of how central music is to the game's mood. Different stations carry different vibes, from upbeat pop to laid-back lounge, and your selection follows your character across stores and vehicles alike.

Taste varies, but the gameplay advice does not: leaving a stereo on silence is the only wrong answer. Even the default stations deliver the customer-experience benefit, so if you never add a single MP3, you still get the full mechanical value — the custom station is a bonus layer for you, the owner, not for the simulation.

## Quick setup checklist

Buy a store stereo at a furniture store, place it in your shop's sales area, add MP3s via Options → Audio → open folder → refresh, select your station from the phone, and check the vibe during one full customer visit. Two minutes of setup for a store that sounds as good as it looks — and if customers are still unhappy, the problem is on our [happiness](/happiness) page, not your playlist.

## Custom music troubleshooting

Three checks solve almost every custom-station problem. Station missing from the list: the folder was filled after the game loaded its stations — reopen the audio options and press refresh, and the files appear. Files present but silent in one store: the stereo is missing or unpowered in that specific shop, since the station selector only controls venues that actually have audio equipment. Music lost after moving computers: expected, because the music folder is local to each machine and is not part of the Steam Cloud save.

If the station plays but skips tracks, the usual culprit is file format rather than the game — stick to standard MP3s and the station behaves. None of this touches your saves, so experimenting with the music folder is as risk-free as modding gets in this game.
