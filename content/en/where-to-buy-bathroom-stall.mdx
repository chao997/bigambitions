Bathroom stalls are bought at furniture stores, in the Business tab of the shop's catalog — that is the whole answer to "where to buy", and the one most players miss because they are looking for a building-supplies store or a special contractor. Walk into a furniture store in Manhattan, browse the commercial section, buy the stall together with a bathroom door and a hand dryer, and install them in your business through build mode.

The reason this is one of the most-searched item questions in the game is that restaurants are required to provide bathrooms once they grow, and the requirements are stricter than most players expect. This page covers where exactly to shop, the rules your bathroom must satisfy, and the traps — like trying to order stalls by phone — that the community runs into constantly.

## Where to buy: furniture stores, Business tab

Manhattan's furniture stores are the single source for bathroom fixtures. The well-known option is Furniture on the Square in Central Manhattan, with smaller furniture shops elsewhere — including along Fifth Avenue — carrying the same catalog. In-store, the items you need live under the Business (commercial) tab rather than the residential sections: bathroom stalls, the bathroom door that makes a room a bathroom, and hand dryers or sinks to complete the set.

Buy the full set while you are there. A stall alone does not satisfy the game's bathroom rules — the room needs the bathroom door to register as a bathroom, and a hand dryer or sink makes it complete for customers. Treat one shopping trip as one bathroom kit and you will not find yourself walking back across Manhattan mid-renovation.

## The rules: when your business needs a bathroom

The rule that triggers this whole search: food businesses with roughly 20 or more seats must provide a customer bathroom. The requirements have three parts — a bathroom room of at least about 12 square meters, a dedicated bathroom door, and at least two bathroom stalls, with a hand dryer or sink strongly recommended. Skimp on any of these and the business does not count as compliant.

Beyond compliance, quantity matters operationally. Customers complain when the ratio of stalls to seating is too tight — the community's recurring example is a 28-seat restaurant limping along on two stalls — and unhappy customers show up exactly where it hurts, in your reviews and footfall. When in doubt, add another stall; the furniture is a one-time cost, while lost customers are a daily one.

## The phone-shopping trap

The most common mistake with bathroom stalls has nothing to do with the stalls: ordering them remotely from your in-game phone frequently does not work — the Business-tab items often fail to appear in the phone's shopping interface, or behave unpredictably. The community's fix is unanimous: shop for bathroom fixtures in person at the furniture store, not over the phone.

It is a small bug-shaped quirk, but it wastes real time for players who build their restaurant, phone-order a stall, and then cannot understand why nothing arrived. If an ordered item seems missing, check whether you are standing inside the furniture store before you conclude the game is broken.

## Placement: build mode and sizing

Installation happens in your business through build mode. Walls define the bathroom as an actual enclosed room — the game checks its size, which is why the ~12 square meter minimum matters before you place a single fixture — then the bathroom door connects it to the customer area, and the stalls and hand dryer go inside. Give customers a short, obvious path from their tables; a bathroom hidden behind the kitchen helps nobody.

One question that comes up constantly: do you need to buy toilet paper or maintain the stalls? No — there is no consumables mechanic for bathrooms in the current game. Once the stalls are installed and the room is compliant, they simply work; the ongoing cost is zero. That makes the bathroom one of the rare purchases in Big Ambitions with no upkeep attached.

When three groups that do not coordinate agree on the numbers, you can build on them.

The phone-shopping quirk is equally well attested, with players repeatedly reporting that Business-tab items misbehave in remote ordering while working fine in-store. Treat this page as the summary of that collective experience — and if 1.0 ever changes the rules, the date at the top of this article is the first thing we will update.

## Quick compliance checklist

For any food business approaching 20 seats: open a furniture store's Business tab (in person), buy at least two stalls, a bathroom door and a hand dryer, build an enclosed room of roughly 12 square meters or more, install everything, and check the result against your seating count. If your restaurant is doubling in size, double-check the stall ratio before the complaints start.

Bathroom compliance is one part of the wider interior-quality system that drives customer satisfaction — the same logic behind the interior scores covered in our [happiness guide](/happiness). Get it right once and it fades into the background of a well-run business, which is exactly where you want it.

## The rest of the same shopping trip

While you are in the Business tab, it is worth finishing the whole interior in one visit, because the same furniture stores stock most of what a food business needs to score well: kitchen equipment and serving fixtures for the dining side, decorations for the interior score that customers and staff both respond to, and the store stereo if you want background music playing. One trip, one delivery, one build-mode session — and the store opens finished instead of accumulating fixtures across a week of errands.

That also matches how the community approaches compliance in general: bathrooms are the mandatory part, but the shopping trip is free, so strong players bundle the nice-to-haves with the must-haves rather than paying for two full shopping cycles.
