Getting a job in Big Ambitions takes about two minutes: open the city map, filter by the job category, walk to an employer and take the job from the prompt on the door. There is no interview, no application form and no waiting period — shifts start as soon as you accept, and your character's schedule fills in around them. The same system works for every job tier in the game, from the grocery store job your Uncle Fred sets up in the story to the high-salary positions that need course qualifications.

This page covers both directions of the jobs system, because "how to get a job" in Big Ambitions means two different things. You can work a job yourself for early income, and once you own businesses you will need to hire employees to run them — which uses a different set of systems entirely.

## Working a job as your character

Early on, the story does the work for you: Uncle Fred's tutorial line lands your character a grocery store job and teaches you how shifts fit into a day. After that first placement, finding work yourself follows the same three steps. Open the map and toggle the job category filter so only employers with open positions appear, travel to the marker that fits you, and accept the job from the interaction prompt.

Qualifications gate the better jobs. Courses you take in the evening — studied at the city's education options — unlock higher salaries and specific career tracks, so the classic early loop is work shifts by day, study courses at night, sleep to advance. Wages land directly in your account, and because the game bills you daily rent and expenses, a steady job is what keeps a pre-business run solvent.

Two practical notes from the community. Shift schedules are fixed, so plan study and errands around them rather than hoping to squeeze both into the same block. And do not neglect the story jobs too early — the campaign uses them to introduce the city, and skipping ahead usually costs more time than it saves.

## Hiring employees for your own business

The moment you open a business, the jobs question flips: now you are the employer. Big Ambitions gives you two hiring systems, and both matter. The first is Anderson Recruitment Corp, the city's recruitment agency — you contact the agency, browse qualified candidates for the roles you need, and hire them directly. It is the reliable way to fill manager, cook, cashier or specialist positions with candidates who meet your requirements.

The second system is the job board, a physical item you place on the wall of your business. It attracts walk-in applicants who apply on their own, which is cheaper but less targeted than the agency. Community guides generally recommend the agency for skilled roles and job boards for high-turnover basic positions, where you mostly need warm bodies at a register.

Staffing is not just headcount. Each employee has wages, a schedule and a workplace they need to actually reach — and their happiness depends on needs like housing, food and transport that they can afford, which ties directly into the [happiness system](/happiness). A full staff that cannot get to work or hates its jobs will underperform one that is half the size and well treated.

## Schedules, managers and letting go

Once staff are hired, the BizMan scheduler handles who works when, and this is where small businesses quietly succeed or fail. Staff your opening hours properly, keep someone on cleaning and restocking, and avoid the common beginner mistake of scheduling everyone at full hours from day one. Labor is a daily cost, and an overstaffed store bleeds money even when it is busy.

As soon as you can afford it, promote or hire a manager and hand over day-to-day operations. A managed business keeps running while your character is across town studying, working or scouting your next location — and that is the point where Big Ambitions stops being a job simulator and starts being the tycoon game it wants to be. Several stores under manager control, feeding profits into your next venture, is the standard mid-game shape of a run.

## The study loop that unlocks better pay

Because qualifications gate salaries, the real early-game strategy is a study loop rather than a job hunt. The pattern the community teaches: hold the best job you currently qualify for, identify the course that unlocks the next tier, and spend evenings enrolled until it completes. Courses cost money and time, but the salary jump after a qualification typically repays both within weeks — and several courses double as prerequisites for business licenses you will want later.

Be realistic about burn. A character who works full days, studies every evening and sleeps minimally will drift into low personal happiness, and the game nudges you back through performance. The sustainable rhythm most players settle on is work, study on most nights, and one evening off for city life — which conveniently doubles as scouting, since walking districts is how you learn where your first store should go.

## Which jobs pay best for your character

If you are working jobs purely for income, follow the qualifications. The community advice is to treat the first in-game weeks as an education arc: take the highest-paying job you qualify for, then enroll in the courses that unlock the next tier up. Business-focused courses also double as prerequisites for running certain business types yourself later, so the study time is never wasted.

There is no need to stay in any job long-term. Unlike career sims, Big Ambitions treats jobs as scaffolding for your real goal — owning businesses. Once your first store is stable and a manager is running it, most players quit their job entirely and live on their businesses, returning to work only if a rebuild ever demands it.

## Can you work a job while owning stores?

Yes, and plenty of runs do exactly that for a while. Jobs and businesses coexist on the same schedule — the conflict is only your character's hours, so the practical version is a part-time shift pattern around store management, or full-time work while a newly hired manager proves themselves. What changes after your first store is the math: once business income covers your daily costs, the wage becomes rounding error, and the hours are usually better spent scouting locations and designing your next interior.

Treat employment as scaffolding rather than a career. The game's progression is measured in businesses owned, and every system — courses, jobs, the recruitment agency, job boards — exists to move you from worker to owner and then to keep your workers happy once you are there.
