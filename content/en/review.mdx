Yes — Big Ambitions is worth playing in 2026, and the numbers back that up. The game holds a "Very Positive" rating on Steam with more than 14,000 reviews at roughly 92% positive as of September 2026, sits around 80 with critics from its Early Access period, and just received its 1.0 full release on August 28, 2026 after three and a half years of public development. This Big Ambitions review sums up what the game is, what the data says, and who should buy it.

The one-line version: it is a role-playing business sim set in a faithfully recreated New York City, where you arrive broke, work jobs, open small businesses and grow them into an empire spanning retail, offices, warehouses and factories. No other game in the genre combines a walkable open-world city with store-by-store business management at this level of detail.

## What the review scores actually say

The Steam data is the strongest signal because the sample is large. Out of 14,075 reviews at the time of checking, 12,993 are positive — 92.3%, which Steam labels "Very Positive". During its Early Access launch week in March 2023 the game debuted in the top ten of Steam's first-week review charts, and industry analyst GameDiscoverCo documented it opening at "Overwhelmingly Positive" with a 95% positive share; the slightly lower overall figure today reflects thousands of later reviews, not a collapse in quality.

Critics landed in the same neighborhood. Metacritic carries critic reviews from the Early Access period at around 80 — Movies Games and Tech scored it 80/100, calling it "a business sim on a level I've not seen before" — and VideoGamesCritic lists it at 85/100. The exact Metacritic metascore for the 1.0 release is still settling as full-release reviews come in, so treat the EA-era numbers as the floor rather than the ceiling. ## What the game does brilliantly

The city is the headline. Manhattan is recreated block by block, and location is a real economic variable — the same store thrives in Midtown and dies in a dead district, which turns every lease into a decision instead of a formality. On top of that sits genuinely deep store management: interior design, blueprints, staffing, schedules, pricing, marketing and supply chains that grow from hand-delivered boxes into warehouses and factories.

The 1.0 release is what tipped the balance for many holdouts. It added The Hamptons with a private mansion, a personal driver, food delivery, an HQ pricing manager, a banking update and minigames, on top of the cinema and theater businesses, blueprint editor and factory overhaul that arrived in late Early Access. After years of watching the roadmap fill in, players finally get the complete shape of the game — and the launch weekend reportedly drew over 12,000 concurrent players.

## The three-and-a-half-year Early Access story

Part of an honest review in 2026 is acknowledging how the game got here. Big Ambitions entered Early Access on March 10, 2023, and unlike many Early Access titles it shipped a public roadmap and then mostly kept to it: optimization and character updates, the Industry City industrial area and product progression system, the Broadway update that added cinemas and theaters plus the blueprint editor, the Workshop update that brought official mod support, and finally 1.0. That cadence is why the community stayed through three and a half years of Early Access — and why the review score held up.

The full release on August 28, 2026 was rewarded with a launch-weekend concurrent-player record the developer celebrated publicly, and the current "Very Positive" rating reflects both long-time players and the wave of new ones the release pulled in. For review purposes the relevant fact is simple: the version you can buy today is the finished shape of the game, not a promise of a finished shape.

## Where it falls short

Big Ambitions respects your time less than it should in the mid-game. Daily routines — sleeping to skip time, driving between businesses, restocking by hand before you automate — are a common complaint in otherwise positive reviews, and the community is candid that some later days feel like waiting for money to accumulate. The simulation is also unforgiving at the start: open a store without a cash cushion and it will fail, which some new players read as the game being broken rather than being strict.

Factories, added late and overhauled in 1.0, remain the most contested system. Community threads range from "three machines under a million dollars and I make smartphones" to "a full factory cannot feed one jewelry store" — the balance is evidently still being tuned. None of these are dealbreakers, but they are the honest answer to "what's the catch".

## Who should buy it

Buy it if you like tycoon and management games with real depth: the loop of scouting a location, building out a store, staffing it and watching profit graphs bend upward is as satisfying here as anywhere in the genre. The game sells for $22.99 on Steam (see our [price guide](/price)), runs on Windows and macOS, and has Steam Workshop support if you want community mods and blueprints on top (see [mods](/mods)).

Skip it or wait if you need PvP, multiplayer, or instant gratification — there is no official co-op, and the first hours are deliberately humble. For everyone else, the verdict of both critics and 14,000 Steam reviewers is roughly the same: Big Ambitions is the most ambitious business sim of its generation, and since 1.0 it is finally finished. ## The verdict in one paragraph

Big Ambitions is the rare simulation that tries something structurally new — a walkable city wrapped around a serious business sim — and then actually finishes the attempt. The Steam data says the audience agrees: 14,000+ reviews at 92% positive, three and a half years of free updates, and a full release that added the lifestyle endgame the whole design was pointing toward. Its flaws are pacing flaws, patchable and patched, not structural ones. If you have read this far, you are the audience.
